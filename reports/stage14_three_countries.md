# Stage 14: one eyelid model for Ghana, India and Italy

Generated by `python -m src.eyelid.three_countries` at commit `f891de1`; settings in `configs/three_countries.yaml`. Can one model trained on all three countries find anemia inside each of them, without guessing the country, the lighting or the photo batch?

- **Photos:** 605: Ghana 388 (42.5% anemic), India 95 (71.6% anemic), Italy 122 (18.9% anemic). One photo per person.
- **Labels, mixed by design:** Ghana children Hb < 11.0 g/dL; India and Italy adults WHO by sex (women < 12.0, men < 13.0 g/dL; pregnancy not recorded).
- **Input:** the 29 color numbers of the hand-segmented palpebral conjunctiva only (no skin, no background).
- **Split:** 5-fold CV x 5 repeats by photo, stratified by country x anemia, scores averaged over repeats. Bootstrap 95% CIs, 2000 resamples. AUROC inside a country can't be helped by knowing the country.
- **Italy early batch:** folders 1-31 (31 photos, 21 anemic), the Italy test that recognizing the batch can't pass (Stage 10).
- **Controls:** `country only` = what knowing the country gives (constant inside a country, so 0.5 there); `no color` = TabPFN on image size and cut-out shape (near 0.5 inside a country = no leak there).
- **Fixes:** per-country standardized = each country's numbers with its own training-fold mean and SD, no labels; reweighing = country and anemia made independent in training (Kamiran and Calders); brightness scaled = every photo scaled to the same mean brightness; lighting jitter = 5 brightness/contrast-jittered copies of each training photo. TabPFN skips reweighing and jitter (no sample weights; > 1000 training rows on CPU).
- **Rank spread:** largest minus smallest mean percentile rank across the three countries, among people with the same anemia status. 0 = the score doesn't depend on the country once anemia status is fixed.
- **Pass, fixed before running:** inside every country, beat the rule and match the country's own model (same folds), with a smaller healthy rank spread than the plain mixed model.
