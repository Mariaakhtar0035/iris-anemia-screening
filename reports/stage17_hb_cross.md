# Stage 17: an Hb model across eyelid datasets, each with its own cutoff

Generated by `python -m src.eyelid.hb_cross` at commit `ba4799f + uncommitted changes`; settings in `configs/hb_cross.yaml`. Mentor advice (2026-10-31): estimate Hb, then apply the local cutoff, so datasets with different cutoffs can share one model.

- **Data:** CP-AnemiC, the 388 photos with no copy (children, **anemic = Hb < 11.0 g/dL**); Eyes-Defy, 217 photos with Hb (adults, **WHO by sex: women < 12.0, men < 13.0 g/dL**, pregnancy not recorded). The cutoffs differ by design; each is applied only to its own dataset.
- **Input:** the 29 color numbers of the palpebral conjunctiva. `as photographed` uses them unchanged; `standardized per dataset` scales each dataset's numbers to mean 0, sd 1 on its own photos (no labels).
- **Hb value rows:** a regressor fit on all of one dataset predicts Hb on the other. Hb error and bias are against the lab Hb. The risk score is the estimate minus the person's own cutoff, so `Sens/Spec at own cutoff` apply each dataset's cutoff to the estimate. `AUROC, ranked by estimate alone` ignores the cutoff (and so sex): it asks only whether a lower estimate goes with anemia. When estimates barely vary, the other AUROC is driven by the cutoff, i.e. by sex (in Eyes-Defy women are more often anemic but have the lower cutoff).
- **anemic yes/no rows:** classifiers fit on the source labels (its own cutoff) and applied unchanged; the untrained rule (minus mean a*) for reference.
- **Italy can't be trusted on its own:** 21 of its 23 anemic patients are in one early photo batch (Stage 10), and that batch alone predicts anemia at AUROC 0.91.
- Bootstrap 95% CIs, 2000 resamples, one photo per person.
