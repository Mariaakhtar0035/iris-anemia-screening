# Stage 16: estimate the Hb value, then apply the cutoff

Generated by `python -m src.eyelid.hb_regression` at commit `ba4799f + uncommitted changes`; settings in `configs/hb_regression.yaml`. Mentor advice from the 2026-10-31 meeting: if you have the Hb, measure both the classification and how well Hb is estimated. Each dataset keeps its own anemia cutoff (stated per section).

- **Input:** the 29 color numbers of the region (eyelid cut-out, or the three nail boxes as photographed). `redness only` uses the mean a* alone, which is what the rule ranks by. The no-color control sees only image size and shape (eyelids) or nail box sizes (nails).
- **`Hb value` rows** are regressors. Their estimate is scored against the lab Hb (MAE, share within 1 g/dL), then used as a risk score (lower estimate = higher risk) for AUROC, PR-AUC, sensitivity at 90% specificity and the share tested to catch 90%. `Sens/Spec at the cutoff` apply the dataset's cutoff to the estimate itself.
- **`anemic yes/no` rows** are the same model families trained on the label directly, for comparison.
- One photo per person. 5x5 CV stratified on the label; the second split holds out a whole hospital (Ghana) or photo session (Moscow) and pools the held-out predictions. Bootstrap 95% CIs, 2000 resamples. For reference, the CP-AnemiC paper's ResNet50 reported an Hb error of 1.54 g/dL on a split with copies on both sides; our grouped replication gave 1.61 (`reports/replicate_cnn.md`).
